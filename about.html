About Us

Who we are, what we believe, and why we started

Our Mission

"USFPRA promotes thoughtful limits, diplomacy, and public accountability in U.S. foreign policy."

The United States Foreign Policy Restriction Association (USFPRA) supports limits on when and how the United States uses military force or gets involved in conflicts abroad. We favor diplomacy and open public debate before any major foreign policy decision.

Our slogan, "Putting National Interest Before Intervention," sums up our main idea. Before the country commits troops, money, and lives overseas, leaders should have to show that it actually serves the American people.

What Kind of Interest Group Are We?

An interest group is an organization of people who share a common goal and try to influence public policy. USFPRA fits three categories.

Public-Interest Group

Public-interest groups work for goals that benefit the whole public, not just their own members. Everyone in the country is affected when the U.S. goes to war: through taxes, prices, safety, and the lives of service members. Better decisions on war and peace help all Americans, whether or not they join us.

Ideological Group

Ideological groups are built around a set of beliefs about how government should work. Ours is foreign policy restraint: the belief that the U.S. should be careful and limited in using military force, and that the constitutional checks on war powers should be respected. We are nonpartisan. Presidents from both parties have expanded war powers without Congress.

Foreign Policy Group

Foreign policy groups focus on how the United States deals with other countries, including war, diplomacy, trade, and national security. That is our entire focus. We work to influence decisions about when the U.S. uses military force abroad, how Congress oversees those decisions, and how much the public is told about them.

What We Believe

1. Focus on trade, diplomacy, peace, and security

The U.S. has the largest economy in the world and huge diplomatic influence. We believe those tools should come first. According to Bridgewater State University political scientist Michael Kryzanek, the U.S. has generally preferred negotiation over military action, and keeping its role as a world leader depends on a foreign policy that pursues peace and avoids war.18

2. Avoid getting pulled into foreign conflicts

Not every crisis overseas is a direct threat to the United States. Long wars like Vietnam and Iraq show how hard it is to get out once troops are committed. Before any intervention, leaders should be able to answer three questions: What is the U.S. interest? What is the goal? How does it end?

3. Military force only in extreme circumstances

We are not a pacifist group. Military action can still be necessary, but only under serious circumstances, such as:

  • Defending the United States, its citizens, or its troops from an attack or a clear and immediate threat
  • Honoring treaty commitments to defend allies, such as NATO
  • Situations where Congress has debated and voted to authorize force

4. Congress and the public should have a say

Article I of the Constitution gives Congress the power "to declare War."1 Congress has not formally declared war since 1942.21 Since then, presidents have often started or expanded military action on their own. We want that to change.

Why College Students Should Care

It could involve us directly

Under the 2026 defense law, the Selective Service plans to start automatically registering men ages 18 to 25 by December 2026.20 There is no draft, but the people any large war would rely on are our age.

We pay for it

The wars after 9/11 cost more than $8 trillion, including veterans' care through 2050.9 Much of that was borrowed, so the interest falls on our generation. The Iran war is already raising gas prices and household costs.8

Our views are different

Polling by the Chicago Council on Global Affairs found that pluralities of Millennials and Gen Z think military approaches are overused, while supporting international cooperation like trade and climate agreements.19
