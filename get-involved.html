Get Involved

Fundraising, campus outreach, and how you can take action

How We Raise Money

Running an interest group costs money. Here is how we plan to fund our work, and what the money is used for.

Fundraising Events

We host events like debate nights, benefit dinners, and charity runs on and near college campuses. Tickets, donations at the door, and merchandise sales (t-shirts, stickers, buttons) all go toward our work.

Online Campaigns

We use online crowdfunding and social media campaigns on Instagram and TikTok to reach small donors across the country. Small, regular donations from many supporters keep us independent.

Partner Organizations

We seek support from established organizations that share our goals, such as groups working on war powers reform, veterans' issues, and civic education. Partners can help with funding, research, and advice.

Where the Money Goes

Every dollar goes toward outreach and advocacy for our policies:

  • Campus outreach: booths, printed flyers, merchandise, and event costs
  • Online outreach: social media content and ads that explain the issues
  • Advocacy: travel for meetings with members of Congress, and research on bills and votes
  • Basic operations: keeping this website running

We believe a group that asks for transparency should be transparent too, so we will share how donations are spent with our members.

On College Campuses

College students are energetic, care about the future, and are some of the people most affected by war. That makes campuses the best place for us to grow.

Booths & Flyers

We set up booths in student centers and at club fairs to talk to students face to face, answer questions, and sign people up. We hand out flyers that explain our cause in one page, with a QR code that links to this website.

Social Media

Most students get their news online. We use TikTok and Instagram to post short videos explaining issues like the Iran conflict and war powers, share updates on votes in Congress, and promote events.

Debates & Discussions

We participate in campus debates and host discussion nights with student political groups from both parties. Foreign policy restraint is an issue where people on the left and right can find common ground.

Campus Chapters

Students who want to do more can start a USFPRA chapter at their school. Chapters run their own booths and events and join our national days of action.

Next Steps: Taking It to Congress

Our goal is to change policy, and that means reaching the people who make it. Here is our plan, and how you can be part of it.

1. Contact your members of Congress

Every American has one representative in the House and two senators. Their offices keep track of what constituents call and write about.

  • Find your representative at house.gov23
  • Find your senators at senate.gov24
  • Or call the U.S. Capitol switchboard at (202) 224-3121 and ask to be connected to their office

2. Send a letter

A short, personal letter or email works best. Here is a template you can use:

Dear [Senator / Representative] [Name],

My name is [your name], and I am a student and constituent from [city, state]. I am writing to ask you to support greater congressional oversight of U.S. military action, including the conflict with Iran. The Constitution gives Congress the power to declare war, and I believe Congress should vote before the United States goes to war. I also ask you to support diplomacy and more transparency about the costs of military operations.

Thank you for your time. Sincerely, [your name]

3. Encourage political participation

We encourage all our members to register to vote, attend town halls, and ask candidates where they stand on war powers. Members of Congress listen most to the people who vote for them.

4. Lobby Congress

As we grow, we will organize trips for members to meet with congressional staff in person. We will bring research, personal stories, and a clear request: vote before war, support diplomacy, and make costs public.

Why Merch and Membership Matter

Interest groups face the free-rider problem: if USFPRA convinces Congress to vote before war, every American benefits, even people who never joined or helped. So why would anyone join?

The answer is selective incentives, benefits that only members get. Our merch and membership are designed to work this way:

  • Material benefit: a tangible reward for joining, like the USFPRA t-shirt or stickers.
  • Solidarity benefit: the sense of belonging that comes from being part of the group, going to events, and working with friends on a cause.
  • Purposive benefit: the satisfaction of working toward a goal you believe in.
